Visual Design Guide: Wuxia-Inspired AI Boxing Coach
Document type: Product theme, art direction, and screen design guidelines
Status: Initial design direction
Scope: PWA visual identity and user interface
Product focus: AI-assisted boxing training, beginning with shadowboxing and basic combinations
1. Design Vision
Create a modern AI boxing coach with the atmosphere of traditional wuxia and Chinese ink-wash art. The visual identity should communicate discipline, focus, movement, and mastery, while the actual coaching experience remains clear, credible, and easy to use.
The design combines two layers:
1. Emotional identity: ink-wash landscapes, brushwork, rice-paper textures, restrained calligraphic details, and red seal motifs.
2. Functional interface: modern typography, clear controls, high-contrast coaching prompts, accessible feedback, and a distraction-free camera experience.
The interface should feel like a contemporary training tool inspired by martial arts traditions, not a fantasy game UI.
Design principle
Ancient martial arts atmosphere. Modern sports-coaching clarity.

2. Art Direction
2.1 Primary style: wuxia ink wash
Use monochrome ink-wash artwork with generous negative space. Preferred visual elements include:
- Expressive black brushwork and soft ink gradients.
- Mist, mountains, clouds, and subtle paper grain.
- Minimal silhouettes of martial artists in recognizable poses.
- Cropped brush marks suggesting movement, impact, or direction.
- Occasional traditional seal-red details.
Artwork should be atmospheric rather than busy. Keep illustrations away from important text and interactive controls.
2.2 Keep the boxing context recognizable
Wuxia is a visual influence, not a change in product category. Users are learning boxing, not sword forms or fictional cultivation techniques.
- Show recognizable boxing guards, jabs, crosses, hooks, footwork, and defensive movements.
- Use real training footage or clear technical diagrams when explaining form.
- Reserve robes, swords, fantasy armor, and elaborate fantasy characters for neither the core UI nor instructional content.
- Do not use an ink illustration as a substitute for evidence from a user's own recording.
2.3 Visual balance
Recommended starting balance across the product:
- 70%: clean interface surfaces, typography, and functional controls.
- 20%: ink-inspired imagery, texture, and brush details.
- 10%: accent color, seals, and decorative flourishes.
These are design targets, not pixel-level requirements. Readability and training safety take priority.
3. Color System
Use a compact palette. Avoid adding extra bright colors without a clear functional reason.
